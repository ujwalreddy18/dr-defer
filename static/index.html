How it works

Four steps. One honest decision.

Most classifiers always answer. DR-Defer adds a gate: if the calibrated confidence falls below a threshold, the case goes to a person.

1. Prepare the image

Crops the black border, pads to a square and resizes, matching how the model was trained.

2. Grade the stage

Three EfficientNet-B0 networks, trained at 512 px, each score the five stages from No DR to Proliferative. Their answers are averaged.

3. Check its confidence

Temperature scaling makes the probabilities honest. Below 50% confidence, it will not answer.

4. Grade or defer

Confident cases are graded with a Grad-CAM heatmap. Uncertain cases go to a specialist.

Evidence

Deferring the hard cases makes the rest more reliable.

Results for the 3-model ensemble on 2,166 held-out EyePACS test images, with APTOS as an external check. Patients never appear in both training and test.

Quality of automatic grades as coverage drops

Coverage is the share of cases the system grades itself. The rest go to a specialist.

What we found

Including the parts that did not work.

Higher resolution helpedTraining at 512 px instead of 384 px raised accuracy from 59.3% to 64.4% (mean of three runs) and QWK from 0.677 to 0.728.
Deferral worksAt 80.5% coverage, accuracy on the cases it grades itself rises from 66.6% to 72.6%, and QWK from 0.749 to 0.815.
The threshold does not transferOn APTOS the same threshold auto-graded 92% of cases, not 80%, and calibration error rose from 0.033 to 0.091. It must be recalibrated for each new clinic.
Fancier scores did not helpDisagreement between the three models was a worse deferral signal than plain confidence: 2.8 accuracy points lower at 80% coverage (95% interval 1.4 to 4.2).

Limits

What this system cannot do.

Knowing the limits is part of the result.

It does not detect diabetes

It grades retinopathy severity in patients who already have diabetes. A "No DR" result does not mean a person is free of diabetes.

Mild and severe cases are the weak spot

Stage 1 is graded correctly only about a third of the time (F1 0.35), and stage 3 is also weak (F1 0.50). Deferral reduces, but does not remove, this.

It answers for any image

A photo that is not a retina still gets a grade. A colour check warns about obvious cases, but confidence alone cannot catch them.

Not accurate enough to screen

It finds 78% of referable cases on EyePACS, below what a screening programme needs. It is for research use only, not a clinical tool.
